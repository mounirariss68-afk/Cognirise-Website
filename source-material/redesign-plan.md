# Cognirise.ai Website Redesign — Strategic Design & Delivery Plan

**Prepared:** 4 September 2026  
**Scope:** research, positioning, design language, design system, homepage and navigation, CMS and technical architecture

## 1. Executive direction

The new Cognirise site should not look like a generic “AI company” and should not behave like a conventional consulting brochure. It should feel like an **executive-grade operating interface for transformation**: predominantly white, precise, editorial, evidence-led, and animated only where motion clarifies how Cognirise works.

### Recommended creative territory: **Visible Intelligence**

A white editorial canvas is structured by fine grid lines, generous space, oversized typographic claims, and controlled Cognirise gradient signals. The gradients should represent intelligence moving through an organization—not decoration. They appear as routes, decision paths, active states, proof markers, and moments of acceleration.

The visual message should be:

- **Strategic enough for a CEO and board**
- **Technical enough for a CIO, CTO, CDO, or architect**
- **Concrete enough for an operations leader**
- **Credible enough for regulated and sovereign environments**
- **Distinct from both dark “AI futurism” and conservative consulting sameness**

The key design principle is **“white space earns trust; evidence earns attention; the gradient signals action.”**

## 2. What the supplied material tells us

### Current site strengths to preserve

- Strong, differentiated language: “AI-native advisory & engineering,” “Forward-deployed people. Forward-deployed agents,” and “Consulting firms leave slides.”
- A substantial offer architecture spanning transformation, foundations, engineering, sovereign AI, and digital workforce.
- Clear sector depth across financial services, government, telecoms, travel, energy, and manufacturing.
- Product/platform substance in CogniOS, CogniAgents, CogniDocs, CogniTalk, and CogniWare.
- A valuable outcome frame: cost, capacity, speed, and risk.

### Current site problems to solve

- The single HTML file contains many page states and a large amount of embedded content; this is difficult to maintain, optimize, govern, and measure.
- Dark fields and dense visual treatments make the brand feel closer to the crowded “futuristic AI” category than to a distinctive executive advisory firm.
- The primary navigation is organization-centric (“Who we are,” “Our Services,” etc.) rather than buyer-task-centric.
- Content depth is high, but hierarchy is uneven. Important proof, points of view, products, and sector propositions compete at similar visual weight.
- Repeated generic CTAs (“Book a demo,” “Talk to us”) do not match visitor intent or stage of consideration.
- Proof should arrive earlier: named outcomes, working systems, case evidence, senior operators, partner credibility, and delivery cadence.
- The current architecture will make upcoming weekly content updates unnecessarily risky and slow.

### What to borrow from the references

- **Oversized, decisive typography:** useful for expressing one strong idea per viewport.
- **Editorial asymmetry and grid discipline:** gives the site a premium, authored quality.
- **White-dominant layouts:** create contrast with the existing category and support executive trust.
- **Proof modules and numeric cards:** useful when backed by Cognirise evidence.
- **Selective black panels:** retain one dark “technical depth” register without allowing it to dominate.
- **Minimal motion with tension:** reveal systems, relationships, and progress rather than adding ambient spectacle.

### What not to copy

- Agency portfolio tropes, decorative orange slabs, fashion photography, tiny all-caps body text, and motion that delays comprehension.
- Brutalism that compromises scanning, accessibility, or executive credibility.
- Generic 3D neural imagery, glowing brains, robots, particles, circuit boards, or synthetic humanoids.

## 3. Research principles for a modern B2B site

Nielsen Norman Group’s B2B research emphasizes that business sites must support long, complex and collaborative buying journeys—not merely collect leads. Buyers need to compare, shortlist, share internally, justify investment, and find technical and business proof. The new site should therefore serve both **choosers** (executives and sponsors) and **users/evaluators** (technical, operational, risk, and procurement stakeholders).

The redesign should follow six evidence-based rules:

1. **Make the proposition understandable in seconds.** Above the fold should answer who Cognirise serves, what it changes, and why its model is different.
2. **Progress from claim to proof.** Each major claim should be followed by evidence: metric, artifact, architecture, case, senior operator, or methodology.
3. **Support multiple buying stages.** Provide fast executive summaries, deeper technical pages, sector plays, downloadable POVs, and low-friction conversation routes.
4. **Design for performance.** Treat LCP, INP, and CLS as design constraints from the first component—not a cleanup task.
5. **Meet WCAG 2.2 AA.** Contrast, focus states, target size, keyboard flow, reduced motion, semantic structure, captions/transcripts, and accessible forms are part of the system.
6. **Make every page maintainable.** Structured CMS fields and reusable page sections should replace hard-coded one-off pages.

## 4. Audience and decision architecture

### Primary audiences

- **Enterprise executive sponsor:** seeks strategic clarity, economic outcomes, senior accountability, and reduced transformation risk.
- **Technology/data leader:** seeks architecture credibility, integration feasibility, sovereignty, security, and a realistic route to production.
- **Operations/business leader:** seeks measurable process change, capacity, speed, and proof that agents can execute—not only assist.
- **Public-sector/regulatory stakeholder:** seeks control, explainability, auditability, local deployment, and responsible governance.

### The homepage’s job

The homepage should not explain everything. It should establish a differentiated belief, demonstrate the operating model, show evidence, and route each visitor toward the most relevant depth.

### Core story arc

1. **Category and promise:** AI-native advisory and engineering that moves from strategy into production value.
2. **The tension:** AI spend is rising, but fragmented pilots, copilots, and slideware do not transform operating performance.
3. **The Cognirise model:** forward-deployed leaders, engineers, and agents working as one accountable team.
4. **The outcomes:** cost, capacity, speed, risk.
5. **Proof:** selected transformations, prototypes, systems, metrics, or artifacts.
6. **Offers:** transformation, foundations, engineering, sovereign AI, digital workforce.
7. **Platforms:** CogniOS ecosystem, explained as an integrated architecture rather than a product catalogue.
8. **Sector relevance:** specific high-value plays by industry.
9. **Thinking and people:** points of view, senior team, advisors, partners.
10. **Conversion:** a concrete first step, preferably “Bring us one process” or “Book a value scan.”

## 5. Design language

### Brand idea

**Intelligence becomes visible when it moves work.**

This gives the site a visual grammar that can extend across photography, diagrams, animation, case studies, and product pages.

### Visual pillars

- **Clarity:** white backgrounds, large hierarchy, direct language, disciplined spacing.
- **Movement:** gradient routes, progress traces, flowing decision paths, subtle state changes.
- **Structure:** fine grids, architectural diagrams, modular cards, labeled evidence.
- **Human authority:** real senior people, environments, workshop details, and documentary imagery—not stock futurism.
- **Operational proof:** process artifacts, interfaces, system maps, before/after timings, and measurable outcomes.

### Color system

- **Canvas:** pure white and soft warm/cool whites for depth.
- **Ink:** Cognirise midnight/navy for primary text and deep technical panels.
- **Gradient spectrum:** violet → magenta → coral/orange, based on the supplied logo colors.
- **Utility neutrals:** blue-gray scale for borders, metadata, inactive controls, and diagrams.
- **Semantic colors:** restrained green, amber, and red used only for status and feedback.

**Rule:** gradient coverage should normally stay below 10–15% of a page. Use it to indicate activation, progression, and high-value interaction. Never place long body copy over a multicolor gradient.

### Typography

Recommended direction: a contemporary grotesk sans for headlines and body, paired optionally with a restrained monospace for architecture labels, evidence tags, and system states. Typography should feel precise rather than trendy.

- Display: 64–104px desktop, tightly tracked, 2–4 line maximum.
- Section title: 40–64px.
- Body lead: 20–26px.
- Body: 17–19px with comfortable line length (approximately 60–75 characters).
- Metadata: 12–14px; never use tiny text to manufacture sophistication.

Font selection should be finalized after testing brand fit, Arabic readiness if required, performance, and licensing.

### Imagery

Build three image families:

- **Human intelligence:** documentary portraits and working moments; direct, quiet, credible.
- **Operational reality:** infrastructure, documents, control rooms, service environments, physical processes.
- **System intelligence:** custom diagrams, data traces, process maps, and abstract gradient interventions derived from real architectures.

Avoid generic AI art. If generative imagery is used, it should be art-directed into a unique Cognirise language and never imply nonexistent client work.

### Motion

- Gradient route draws to show work moving through a system.
- Number and state transitions to show before/after improvement.
- Layered architecture reveals controlled by scrolling or user input.
- Short page transitions and restrained hover feedback.
- Full reduced-motion alternative.

No autoplay hero video is recommended for version one; it adds performance cost before the visual system has earned it.

### Voice and microcopy

- Lead with a point of view, not a category adjective.
- Prefer specific verbs: build, deploy, govern, integrate, reduce, shorten.
- Use short declarative headlines and evidence-rich supporting copy.
- Replace vague CTAs with intent-specific actions: “Explore the operating model,” “See banking plays,” “Bring one process,” “Read the architecture,” “Book a value scan.”

## 6. Design system plan

### Foundations

- Color tokens: canvas, ink, neutral scale, brand spectrum, semantic states.
- Type tokens: display through caption, responsive using fluid clamp values.
- Spacing: 4px base with a disciplined 8/12/16/24/32/48/64/96/144 scale.
- Grid: 12-column desktop, 8-column tablet, 4-column mobile; max content width around 1440px.
- Radius: minimal and purposeful; avoid default “SaaS rounded card” styling.
- Borders: fine, high-quality dividers; slightly stronger for interactive states.
- Shadows: rare; use layering, borders, and tonal contrast first.
- Motion: fast UI state, standard reveal, and narrative sequence tokens.

### Core components

- Global header, mega menu, mobile navigation, footer.
- Buttons: primary gradient, secondary ink, tertiary text/arrow, inverse.
- Editorial hero, sector hero, platform hero, article hero.
- Outcome strip and metric/evidence cards.
- Service cards, sector play cards, platform relationship map.
- Case-study teaser, quote, timeline, before/after, architecture diagram.
- Insight cards, filters, related-content rails, author block.
- Partner/advisor profile modules.
- CTA band, contact form, newsletter form.
- Accordions, tabs, modal/dialog, breadcrumb, pagination.
- Rich text styles, tables, code/technical labels, download blocks.

### Component states and governance

Every interactive component requires default, hover, focus-visible, active, disabled, loading, success, and error states where relevant. Each CMS block should have a documented purpose, content limits, responsive behavior, accessibility notes, and prohibited uses.

### Design-system deliverables

- Figma variables and styles.
- Component library with responsive variants.
- Token source shared with code.
- Pattern library / Storybook.
- Accessibility annotations.
- Content guidelines and component usage rules.
- QA checklist and visual-regression coverage.

## 8. Homepage blueprint

### 1. Header

White, calm, compact. Logo left; navigation center/right; gradient CTA. Sticky after initial scroll, with clear keyboard behavior.

### 2. Hero — one decisive belief

**Working headline direction:** “AI should move the business—not just assist it.”  
Support: “Cognirise is the AI-native advisory and engineering firm. Senior operators, forward-deployed engineers, and governed agents move priority work from strategy into production.”

Primary CTA: **Bring us one process**  
Secondary CTA: **See how we work**

Visual: a clean organizational workflow that begins inert, then activates along a Cognirise gradient route. This is more ownable and informative than a stock hero image.

### 3. Proof bar

A concise row of verified proof: delivery speed, named deployments, sectors, partners, or measurable outcomes. Do not publish placeholder statistics.

### 4. Problem / point of view

“Billions are being spent on AI. Too little work is changing.” Contrast fragmented pilots and copilots with governed, end-to-end operating change.

### 5. Operating model

Interactive but accessible diagram: **Forward-deployed people + forward-deployed agents**. Show discovery, prototype, production, scale, and governance as one loop.

### 6. Outcomes

Four large modules: reduce cost, create capacity, shorten time-to-market, de-risk transformation. Each should link to relevant proof.

### 7. Selected work / proof

Two or three detailed stories, even if initially anonymized. Structure each as problem → intervention → system → result. A real artifact is better than a decorative image.

### 8. What we do

Five service areas arranged as a connected system rather than equal generic cards.

### 9. Platform ecosystem

One integrated visual showing CogniOS and specialist capabilities. Allow progressive disclosure for technical evaluators.

### 10. Industry plays

Sector selector with concrete use cases. Each panel should answer: where value leaks, what Cognirise deploys, and the likely first engagement.

### 11. Senior-led team

A compact, credible leadership section with direct role relevance—not a large portrait gallery.

### 12. Insights

Three editorial items with strong titles, reading time, sector/topic tags, and a clear “View all insights.”

### 13. Conversion

“Start with one day. Leave with a business case.” Explain what the session includes, who should attend, and what the visitor receives.

### 14. Footer

Full navigation, platform/industry links, contact, legal, newsletter, social, and a compact brand statement.

## 9. CMS architecture

### Recommended model

Use a headless CMS with structured content and visual preview. The front end should render reusable sections while protecting design quality. Editors should assemble approved blocks—not freestyle arbitrary page layouts.

**Recommended shortlist:** Sanity, Contentful, or Storyblok. Sanity is the initial recommendation because its schema flexibility, references, structured portable text, preview workflows, and custom editorial studio fit a content-rich advisory/platform site. Final choice should account for the client team’s procurement, hosting, roles, localization, and governance requirements.

### Content types

- Page
- Service
- Platform
- Industry
- Case study
- Insight / article
- Point-of-view / downloadable report
- Person
- Partner
- Event / news item
- Global settings
- Navigation
- SEO metadata
- Redirect
- Form configuration

### Reusable content objects

- Hero
- Rich text section
- Claim + evidence
- Metric group
- Outcome module
- Quote/testimonial
- Case teaser
- Service/platform/industry references
- Architecture diagram
- Timeline/process
- Comparison/before-after
- Media with caption/alt text
- CTA band
- FAQ
- Related content
- Download gate

### Essential relationships

- Services ↔ industries ↔ platforms ↔ case studies ↔ insights.
- People ↔ services / industries / authored insights.
- Partners ↔ capabilities / platform integrations.
- Each page receives curated related content with an automatic fallback by taxonomy.

### Editorial workflow

Draft → internal review → legal/compliance review where required → scheduled publish → update reminder → archive. Include role-based permissions, preview links, revision history, and explicit owners/review dates for claims and metrics.

### Content governance

- Every statistic stores source, source URL, verification date, and expiry/review date.
- Every media asset requires alt text or a deliberate decorative flag.
- Every article has author, date, updated date, topic, sector, and reading time.
- Every case study identifies what can be public, anonymized, or restricted.
- Avoid duplicating copy in fields; use references and canonical source objects.

## 10. Technical architecture

### Recommended stack

- **Frontend:** Next.js with TypeScript.
- **Rendering:** static generation / incremental regeneration for marketing pages; server rendering only where necessary.
- **CMS:** Sanity as provisional recommendation.
- **Hosting:** managed edge hosting with preview deployments and rollback.
- **Search:** begin with CMS-powered search; add Algolia only if the content library justifies it.
- **Forms:** server-side validation, spam protection, CRM/email routing, consent logging.
- **Analytics:** privacy-conscious analytics plus consent management and event taxonomy.
- **Monitoring:** uptime, error tracking, Core Web Vitals, broken-link checks, and visual regression.

### Performance targets

- Core Web Vitals “good” thresholds at the 75th percentile.
- LCP ≤ 2.5s, INP ≤ 200ms, CLS ≤ 0.1.
- Minimal client-side JavaScript for content pages.
- Responsive images (AVIF/WebP), explicit dimensions, lazy loading below the fold.
- Self-hosted/subset fonts where licensing permits.
- No heavy animation library unless the interaction genuinely requires it.

### SEO and discoverability

- Semantic HTML, one clear H1, logical heading structure.
- Structured data for Organization, Person, Article, Breadcrumb, and relevant services.
- XML sitemap, robots controls, canonical URLs, Open Graph metadata.
- Server-rendered content for all essential copy.
- Topic clusters connecting services, sectors, work, and insights.
- Redirect map and pre/post-launch crawl.
- Content designed to be quotable by search and AI answer systems: clear definitions, evidence, named authors, updated dates, and strong internal linking.

### Security and compliance baseline

- Strict content security policy, secure headers, dependency monitoring.
- Role-based CMS access and SSO if required.
- Minimal third-party scripts.
- Data minimization for forms and documented retention.
- Accessibility, privacy, and cookie behavior reviewed before launch.

## 11. Research and design workplan

### Phase 0 — Alignment and evidence collection (3–5 days)

- Confirm business goals, launch scope, target markets, languages, and conversion priorities.
- Inventory all current pages, copy, claims, assets, analytics, backlinks, and planned updates.
- Interview 5–8 stakeholders across leadership, delivery, sales, and technology.
- Gather 3–5 recent buying stories or lost opportunities using a JTBD-style timeline.
- Identify proof that can be published: outcomes, artifacts, client permissions, partner logos, quotes.

**Output:** brief, content inventory, measurement baseline, proof register, decision log.

### Phase 1 — Research and strategy (1–2 weeks)

- Benchmark 10–15 AI, engineering, transformation, and premium professional-services sites.
- Review usability, accessibility, performance, messaging, content depth, and conversion patterns.
- Map audience questions and buying stages.
- Finalize positioning hierarchy, sitemap, and content model.

**Output:** research synthesis, audience/decision map, differentiated creative brief, sitemap, CMS model.

### Phase 2 — Design language exploration (1 week)

Create three distinct but on-brand directions, all predominantly white:

1. **Visible Intelligence** — recommended: operational traces, gradient pathways, editorial authority.
2. **Executive Field Manual** — bolder typographic editorialism, more black/white contrast, minimal imagery.
3. **Living Architecture** — diagram-led, modular, more technical and product-forward.

Evaluate each against differentiation, executive trust, technical credibility, content flexibility, accessibility, and performance.

**Output:** moodboards, type/color/motion studies, homepage key frames, recommendation.

### Phase 3 — UX and content design (1–2 weeks)

- Wireframe homepage and priority templates.
- Rewrite navigation and CTA system.
- Define proof placement and conversion paths.
- Prototype menu, architecture explainer, and one industry story.
- Test with 5 representative users/stakeholders.

**Output:** tested wireframes, content hierarchy, page-level briefs, revised prototype.

### Phase 4 — Design system and visual design (2–3 weeks)

- Build foundations, tokens, components, responsive behavior, and accessibility states.
- Design homepage plus priority templates at desktop and mobile.
- Create diagram, imagery, and motion rules.
- Document CMS block behavior.

**Output:** production design system, approved templates, motion prototypes, asset direction.

### Phase 5 — Build and CMS implementation (3–5 weeks)

- Build front end and CMS schemas.
- Set up preview, workflows, redirects, forms, SEO, analytics, and monitoring.
- Migrate and restructure approved content.
- Run accessibility, performance, browser, device, and content QA.

**Output:** staging site, populated CMS, QA reports, editor training.

### Phase 6 — Launch and optimization (1 week + ongoing)

- Final crawl and redirect validation.
- Launch with rollback plan.
- Monitor errors, vitals, rankings, and conversion events.
- Run 30/60/90-day content and conversion review.

**Output:** live site, launch report, prioritized optimization backlog.

## 12. Measurement framework

### Primary measures

- Qualified contact/value-scan requests.
- Conversion by audience entry point, service, and industry page.
- Case-study and architecture engagement.
- Return visits and internal sharing proxies.
- Organic visibility for strategic service/industry topics.

### Quality measures

- Core Web Vitals pass rate.
- WCAG 2.2 AA audit result.
- Task completion in usability tests.
- CMS publishing time and content error rate.
- Percentage of key claims supported by current evidence.

## 13. Decisions needed before visual design begins

- Primary commercial conversion: value scan, working session, demo, or general conversation.
- Priority audiences and geographies for launch.
- Whether Platforms are sold independently or primarily strengthen advisory credibility.
- Which client outcomes, names, artifacts, and metrics can be public.
- Launch languages and Arabic requirements.
- CRM, newsletter, analytics, and consent platforms.
- CMS procurement/security constraints.
- Content owners and approval workflow.

## 14. Immediate next move

Proceed with a **Design exploration project** covering the three visual territories, with **Visible Intelligence** as the lead direction. Start by converting the supplied content into an inventory and designing four key frames: homepage hero, operating model, proof/case module, and platform architecture. This will resolve the highest-risk decisions—brand distinctiveness, proof hierarchy, and the balance between advisory authority and engineering depth—before the full component library or build begins.
