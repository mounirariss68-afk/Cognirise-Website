# Real-world AI starting situations

**Research date:** 14 September 2026  
**Depth:** Deep  
**Sources cited:** 25  
**Status:** Recommendations for user review—not approved website copy  
**Boundary:** No website, CMS, framework, assessment or design changes.

## Executive recommendation

Replace the six current method-shaped labels with **seven recognizable business situations**, organized around the next decision the organization needs to make. Keep **situation → relevant decision method → IDAO**, but do not require a separate assessment when the buyer can enter IDAO directly.

My recommended wording is:

| | Proposed starting situation | Proposed explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **We need to know where AI is worth investing.** | We are exploring AI, but have not established a useful opportunity or a convincing business case. We need to understand the likely benefits, costs and evidence before committing. |
| 2 | **We have several AI ideas and need to choose.** | We have competing proposals and limited money or capacity. We need to decide what to fund first, what to investigate and what to stop. |
| 3 | **We have an AI strategy and need to implement it.** | We already have a strategy or roadmap, whether developed internally or by a consultant. We need to turn it into owned, funded delivery work without repeating decisions that are already supported. |
| 4 | **We need to improve a specific process.** | We know where work is slow, costly or unreliable. We need to work out whether AI would help and what must change in the process—not start by assuming it needs agents. |
| 5 | **We have a pilot and need to put it into everyday use.** | We have tested something, but it is not yet a supported part of normal work. We need to establish what remains before people can rely on it. |
| 6 | **AI works in one area. We need to expand it.** | We have evidence of value in an existing live setting. We need to decide what can be reused and what must change for other teams, locations or workloads. |
| 7 | **Our AI is in use, but the results are falling short.** | AI is already part of the work, but the benefits, quality, cost or adoption are disappointing. We need to identify the cause and decide whether to improve, redesign, replace or stop it. |

These are **proposed buyer-language descriptions**, not quotations from customers or statistically validated market segments. Seven is an editorial recommendation: it preserves material differences in the immediate decision without giving every cause of difficulty its own entry.

Use **strategy, operations and implementation as secondary context**, not three compulsory front-door categories. Someone with a roadmap and a failing pilot has all three kinds of concern. The useful distinction is what they need resolved now.

The two most important changes are explicit entry for business-case work and explicit entry for an existing strategy. Neither should default to a maturity assessment. A strategy document is useful evidence to preserve, but it is not evidence that a solution works. Conversely, a working solution is no reason to repeat strategy.

## 1. What was researched—and what the evidence can establish

The research screened 39 distinct sources across official surveys and guidance, academic studies, public procurement, industry research and supplier commentary. The cited set below contains 25 sources selected for substantive evidence or an explicit methodological comparison. Saved evidence and a register accompany this report.

This was desk research. It did not include interviews, paid databases, user testing or contact with organizations. The research supports recurring problems and practical distinctions. It does not establish how many Cognirise buyers belong in each proposed situation, whether these exact words improve conversion, or an optimal number of choices.

Evidence is treated according to what it can prove. A procurement notice establishes a stated requirement, not successful delivery. A survey reports its respondents' answers, not a causal return on investment. A field experiment can support effects in its studied setting, not every industry. Supplier commentary can help explain a mechanism but is not independent proof of buyer prevalence.

### Finding A: “Should we invest?” and “Which proposal comes first?” are different decisions

The OECD's review of firm surveys includes difficulty identifying appropriate use cases and demonstrating value over alternatives among the barriers investigated. It also records concerns about skills, data, integration and cost; these are not reducible to a single maturity label. [[1]](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/the-adoption-of-artificial-intelligence-in-firms_f9ef33c3-en/full-report/comparisons-among-recent-ai-surveys_6577dea3.html) The UK government's AI Adoption Research, published in February 2026, used 3,500 business interviews and 100 follow-up interviews. It identifies lack of an identified need and limited skills as important blockers, and its qualitative evidence includes difficulty making a compelling business case. These are UK findings, not a GCC demand estimate. [[2]](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-adoption-research/ai-adoption-research)

CSIRO's investment guide explicitly frames AI projects as problem-focused investments: evaluate strategic fit, measurable value, full costs, data, integration and risk, including whether a non-AI option is preferable. It also distinguishes project evaluation from ongoing portfolio choices to continue, pivot or stop. [[3]](https://www.csiro.au/-/media/D61/Investing-in-AI-projects-guide/2025-Evaluating-and-prioritising-AI-projects-.pdf) Together, these sources support two entry decisions: establish a defensible opportunity and investment case; or compare already-articulated candidates under resource constraints. They do not show that every organization needs an AI strategy engagement.

The size boundary matters. OECD's SME synthesis discusses skills, financing and organizational change, and reports that some non-users see generative AI as unsuitable for their work. [[4]](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/12/ai-adoption-by-small-and-medium-sized-enterprises_9c48eae6/426399c1-en.pdf) Eurostat's 2025 data distinguish company sizes and report expertise, legal and privacy barriers among firms that considered AI but did not adopt it; its population excludes enterprises below ten people. Those conditional results cannot describe all nonadopters or all microbusinesses. [[5]](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Use_of_artificial_intelligence_in_enterprises) The recommendation therefore includes a credible “not AI,” “not yet” or “do not fund” outcome instead of treating hesitation as a deficiency to correct.

There is also buying-process evidence. CIO Dive's reporting of G2's 2025 buyer survey describes stricter requirements for AI software and demand for demonstrated value before paying a premium. Because this is secondary reporting of a commercial survey, it supports the relevance of investment justification, not a forecast of Cognirise conversion. [[6]](https://www.ciodive.com/news/AI-enterprise-procurement-purchasing-data-G2/748369/)

### Finding B: There is a real strategy-to-delivery handoff—but the exact consultant-origin case must not be overstated

London CIV's June 2026 public tender is the closest direct buyer evidence found. It describes a separately procured AI Strategy programme intended to produce a prioritized portfolio, then seeks an implementation partner for feasibility refinement, design, build, integration, governance, training and support. The strategy was described as recently initiated: this is evidence of a planned handoff, **not** proof that a completed consultant-authored roadmap had already been delivered, or that the implementation succeeded. The fetched notice is also an older version. [[7]](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/057908-2026)

Other procurement evidence reinforces the distinction between choosing a direction and supplying delivery capability. The UK Department for Business and Trade's AI³ procurement distinguishes exploration in an AI Lab, operationalization in an AI Factory, and maintenance/scaling in AI Operations. These are that buyer's terms, not stages to import into Cognirise. [[8]](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/procurement/ocds-h6vhtk-0589f1) A Home Office award procures AI infrastructure delivery support; it is evidence of actual delivery purchasing, but does not establish a previous consultant strategy. [[9]](https://www.contractsfinder.service.gov.uk/notice/3fd991c0-ad8e-4982-9eed-067274197845)

UK AI procurement guidance calls for multidisciplinary teams, data discovery, problem-based requirements and lifecycle support, including knowledge transfer and training. Although the guidance is historical, its relevance here is the nature of the work needed beyond an agreed ambition—not a current adoption statistic or legal opinion. [[10]](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guidelines-for-ai-procurement/guidelines-for-ai-procurement)

**Recommendation:** retain the user's exact post-strategy need as a visible entry. The empirical record supports the handoff, while the consultant-origin/completed-roadmap detail is supplied by the user rather than measured by this research. Start by establishing what is already decided and evidenced. Reopen only material gaps or changed assumptions. Do not relabel another discovery-and-strategy exercise as implementation.

### Finding C: Buyers can start with an operational problem, not an AI initiative

Workplace studies show why “one workflow” is too imprecise. The NBER customer-support study examines a real deployment and finds different effects for workers with different experience and skill levels. Its older working-paper version is used here for that mechanism, not a current universal productivity percentage. [[11]](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31161/w31161.pdf?ck=1745495794) The Alibaba customer-service field experiment likewise examines specific task support and heterogeneous outcomes, rather than establishing that an assistant improves every aspect of performance for every worker. It is a working paper/preprint, not conclusive cross-industry evidence. [[12]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.29888v1)

Melbourne Business School's 2026 whitepaper emphasizes task and workflow redesign, decision rights, learning and the risk of mistaking more output for better outcomes. This is a qualitative synthesis and leadership discussion, not a controlled trial. [[13]](https://mbs.edu/-/media/PDF/Centres/IDIA/AI--the-Redesign-of-Work-IDIA-Whitepaper-June-2026.pdf?rev=e44d8f5537b34a3d9f3af6af74c81d31) Across these sources, the defensible inference is that the work, people and outcome measure matter—not that every slow process needs an autonomous agent.

A buyer saying “invoice exceptions take too long” has supplied an operational problem. They may not yet know whether the answer is AI, simpler automation, better data or a process change. That is meaningfully different from “we have a claims AI pilot and need to release it.” Both concern one workflow, but they need different decisions and different evidence.

### Finding D: A demonstration, a live service and a scalable service are not interchangeable

RAND's qualitative research identifies mechanisms including misunderstood problems, data difficulties, unsuitable deployment environments and weak operational infrastructure. Its 2024 report is older than 18 months; it is used for mechanisms, not a current AI failure rate. [[14]](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA2680-1.html) Cisco's 2025 survey addresses operational infrastructure, scaling and impact measurement among larger organizations; this is vendor-sponsored self-reporting, not independent certification of readiness. [[15]](https://www.cisco.com/c/dam/m/en_us/solutions/ai/readiness-index/2025-m10/documents/cisco-ai-readiness-index-2025-realizing-the-value-of-ai.pdf)

McKinsey's GCC research, based on 139 executives/board directors and 14 interviews, distinguishes reported adoption from wider deployment and its own definition of value realization. It also discusses foundations, operating models and change management. [[16]](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai-in-gcc-countries-in-pursuit-of-scale-and-value) Deloitte's 2026 Middle East reporting similarly discusses production deployment, workflow/role redesign and governance. The release does not give enough detail to use its regional percentages as a population benchmark here. [[17]](https://www.deloitte.com/middle-east/en/about/press-room/deloitte-unveils-new-era-of-enterprise-ai-as-middle-east-organizations-shift-from-pilots-to-large-scale-deployment.html)

IBM's commentary describes a plausible transition problem: curated pilot data and manual review do not remove the need for integrated live data, governance and actions in systems of record. This is a supplier explanation, used alongside the stronger sources rather than as independent prevalence evidence. [[18]](https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/why-most-enterprise-ai-projects-stall-before-scale)

The practical recommendation is to distinguish **first dependable use**, **expansion from proven use**, and **repair of disappointing use**. These are different next decisions, even though the same team may face all three across different initiatives. A pilot is not automatically ready for Activate; existing deployment is not automatically proof of sustained value.

### Finding E: Adoption and value are substantive needs, but do not always need their own starting category

The UAE evidence makes people and adoption impossible to treat as a final training activity. Red Hat's UAE survey emphasizes adoption plans and skills gaps; it is a vendor-sponsored survey and not a representative measure of all UAE firms. [[19]](https://www.redhat.com/en/about/press-releases/red-hat-survey-uae-organisations-prepare-for-widespread-ai-adoption-corrections-skills-gaps) PwC's UAE workforce research discusses employee use, confidence and upskilling; employee perceptions should not be confused with organizational readiness or audited business outcomes. [[20]](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/issues/pdf/hopes-and-fears-uae-2025.pdf) KPMG's older UAE report includes organizational accounts such as Al Ghurair and Etihad alongside discussion of policy, processes and workforce change. These are attributed accounts, not independently verified ROI cases. [[21]](https://assets-launch.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/ae/pdf/future-of-work.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf)

Value measurement is also not interchangeable with adoption measurement. Wharton's 2025 sponsored, academic-affiliated commercial survey investigates ROI tracking and reported returns within its US enterprise sample; the answers are self-reported, not audited or causal ROI evidence. [[22]](https://ai.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2025-Wharton-GBK-AI-Adoption-Report_Full-Report.pdf) ServiceNow/Oxford Economics examines strategy, workflow integration, talent, governance and value realization in a different vendor-sponsored, self-reported survey; its maturity findings are not independent benchmarks or causal evidence. [[23]](https://www.servicenow.com/content/dam/servicenow-assets/public/en-us/doc-type/resource-center/white-paper/wp-enterprise-ai-maturity-index-2025.pdf) PwC's 2026 proprietary Saudi survey focuses on moving from maturity toward value but has only 35 senior respondents at large organizations: useful directional self-reporting, not a Saudi population estimate or causal value study. [[24]](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/publications/saudi-arabia-ai-maturity-value.html)

These sources differ in population, definitions and questions. Their headline percentages should not be averaged or compared as if they measured one adoption funnel. BCG's GCC AI Pulse is a further example: its country-level readiness framework is relevant regional context, but is not an enterprise buyer-needs survey and is not used to rank the proposed situations. [[25]](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/the-gcc-ai-pulse-mapping-the-regions-readiness-for-an-ai-driven-future)

**Recommendation:** make adoption, responsibilities, skills and control explicitly discoverable within implementation, expansion and underperformance. For now, do not add an eighth general entry called “human–agent work design.” An organization that specifically wants role and responsibility redesign should still be able to go directly to the Human–Agent Operating Model. If the user considers this a major standalone buying need, a separate plain-language entry is a reasonable alternative to review—not a conclusion already validated by this research.

## 2. Why the current six situations are confusing

This is a source-code/content audit, not an observed usability study. The current list mixes **scope** (“organization-wide,” “one workflow”), **quantity** (“multiple opportunities”), **specialist concepts** (“human–agent work design,” “handover authority”), and **a type of evidence** (“live operation”). A buyer with several failing workflows fits almost all of them.

The near one-to-one correspondence with existing methods also asks the visitor to understand the methodology portfolio before describing their own need. “Specific handover authority” may be exact terminology inside the canonical framework, but it is not a general-purpose business starting situation.

| Current situation | Recommended treatment | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Organization-wide constraint | Replace; distribute between investment, existing strategy, expansion and underperformance | Organization-wide scope does not say what decision is blocked. Value-to-Scale remains relevant when the constraint is genuinely systemic. |
| Multiple opportunities | Retain the underlying need; rewrite as situation 2 | Having several ideas is meaningful when the immediate problem is choosing under budget/capacity limits. |
| One use case or workflow | Split by the need: process improvement, pilot release, expansion or repair | A bounded scope does not establish whether the buyer is diagnosing, testing, deploying or fixing. Do not assume agents. |
| Human–agent work design | Move from general entry label to explicit relevant decision work and specialist access | Preserve the method and its value; translate the need into responsibilities, adoption, skills and changed work. |
| Specific handover authority | Preserve specialist access and contextual relevance; remove as a general buyer category | A safety or authority owner may need this directly. Most buyers should not have to understand it first or pass through it without a consequential handover. |
| Evidence from live operation | Split into expansion and disappointing results | Evidence is an input; the buyer's decision is whether/how to extend successful use or address an existing problem. |

## 3. How to organize the recommended set

Three plausible alternatives were considered:

| Organizing approach | Advantage | Main problem | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategy / operations / implementation | Familiar service language | Not mutually exclusive; it hides business-case versus roadmap work, and a process improvement can require all three | Use as explanatory context only |
| Explore / pilot / deploy / scale | Short and easy to order | Implies a journey everyone follows; obscures bought strategy, funding choices and repair; does not identify the actual blocked decision | Do not use as the primary categories |
| Immediate business decision, expressed in recognizable situations | Makes current assets and next action explicit; accommodates later entry and stopping | Requires short clarifiers where needs overlap | Recommended |

The common axis is **the decision the buyer wants resolved now**. References to a roadmap, pilot or live service are recognition cues and evidence to inspect, not a maturity score. Company size, industry, geography, number of workflows and AI technology are context—not separate levels of the taxonomy.

The proposed order supports reading from uncertain investment through delivery to operation, but it is **not a prescribed sequence** or ranking of market prevalence. A reader can start at any situation. When several apply, identify the initiative or work package and choose its most immediate decision; do not pretend the whole organization has one permanent state.

Seven is not a scientifically optimal number. Merging investment-case and prioritization work would reduce the count to six, but would hide the difference between “is this worth doing?” and “which of these should come first?” Adding separate entries for data, procurement, skills, governance and tooling would increase overlap because those can be causes in several situations. The recommended seven retain the most consequential decision differences while keeping those causes explicit within the response.

## 4. Detailed starting-situation definitions

All examples in this section are **constructed illustrations**, not claims about particular clients. Desired outputs below describe the business need; they do not claim that every output already exists as a self-service Cognirise tool.

### 1. We need to know where AI is worth investing.

**Trigger and existing assets:** Leadership is interested in AI, or asks for a funding proposal, but the team lacks a credible opportunity or investment case. It may have strategic goals, cost pressures, baseline information and a few ideas—not necessarily a chosen project.

**Immediate question:** What problem, if any, is worth addressing with AI, and what evidence would justify the investment?

**Practical outcome:** A bounded value hypothesis; comparison with non-AI alternatives; benefits, full costs, assumptions, owner and measurement plan; a proportionate next test or a reason not to proceed. A complete finance-approved business case may require additional analysis beyond the published methods.

**Example:** A mid-sized distributor is under pressure to “use AI,” but cannot explain which investment would improve service or margin.

**Include/exclude:** Include an unproven investment case for one idea. Exclude a developed set of comparable proposals when the immediate decision is their funding order.

**Clarifier:** “Do we still need to establish the case, or mainly choose between proposals we already understand?” Evidence basis: investment and nonadoption findings in section 1A.

### 2. We have several AI ideas and need to choose.

**Trigger and existing assets:** Functions submit competing proposals; leadership has a budget or delivery-capacity constraint. Candidate descriptions, value hypotheses and some feasibility information exist, even if evidence quality varies.

**Immediate question:** Which proposals should advance, in what order, and which should wait or stop?

**Practical outcome:** A transparent comparative decision with evidence gaps, capacity/control considerations, and investigate, demonstrate, activate or stop recommendations. A score is not a funding approval or validated ROI.

**Example:** A bank has proposals for knowledge search, service assistance and document processing, but capacity for one initial delivery team.

**Include/exclude:** Include conflicting priorities inside an existing strategy. Exclude a roadmap whose priorities are already supported and whose problem is delivery ownership or execution.

**Clarifier:** “Is the main obstacle choosing the work, or delivering work already chosen?” Evidence basis: CSIRO portfolio evaluation and procurement/value evidence in section 1A.

### 3. We have an AI strategy and need to implement it.

**Trigger and existing assets:** A strategy/roadmap exists, from internal work or a consultant, but has not become practical delivery. The assets can include priority use cases, architecture, recommendations and a governance model; their actual completeness varies.

**Immediate question:** What can we start delivering now, who owns it, and what evidence or dependencies are still missing?

**Practical outcome:** An agreed first delivery package, accountable owner, funding/resourcing decisions, acceptance measures, dependencies and evidence-based IDAO entry. Preserve sound prior decisions; isolate gaps rather than commission strategy again by default.

**Example:** A utilities organization has a consultant's roadmap but no owner, funded team or implementation scope for the first use case.

**Include/exclude:** Include a roadmap with incomplete execution detail. If the buyer instead wants to challenge its priorities, use situation 2; if a chosen pilot is already awaiting deployment, situation 5 is more precise.

**Clarifier:** “Is the block turning the roadmap into work, or a specific pilot/implementation already underway?” Evidence basis: section 1B; the exact completed consultant-origin scenario is user-specified, not measured prevalence.

### 4. We need to improve a specific process.

**Trigger and existing assets:** A known operational problem is causing delay, errors, cost or poor service. The team can identify affected work and ideally a process owner and baseline, but has not established an appropriate intervention.

**Immediate question:** What change would improve this process, and does AI have a useful role?

**Practical outcome:** A clear problem and baseline, changed-work proposal, AI/non-AI options, feasibility questions and a bounded test. Agent readiness and authority come later only if relevant.

**Example:** An insurer wants fewer manual claims exceptions; a hospitality team wants faster resolution of guest-service requests.

**Include/exclude:** Include a painful process with no AI hypothesis yet. Exclude an existing AI solution whose results need repair—use situation 7—or a pilot ready for release consideration—use situation 5.

**Clarifier:** “Are we deciding what intervention would help, or already trying to deploy/fix a particular AI solution?” Evidence basis: workflow and worker-context studies in section 1C.

### 5. We have a pilot and need to put it into everyday use.

**Trigger and existing assets:** A prototype or limited trial exists. The organization still needs evidence, integration, security, support, ownership, adoption or a safe release decision before routine use.

**Immediate question:** What must be proven or completed before this can become a dependable part of normal work?

**Practical outcome:** An explicit proceed/prepare/stop decision where the agent-readiness method applies; otherwise an IDAO gate decision and release work covering acceptance, integration, monitoring, fallback, support and people. A demo does not automatically earn release.

**Example:** A document-processing pilot works on a curated sample but is not integrated with live records or exception handling.

**Include/exclude:** Include a successful-looking trial that has not demonstrated sustained operational value. Exclude an established live service with that evidence whose next decision is expansion.

**Clarifier:** “Is this already relied on in normal work, with owned support and demonstrated results?” Evidence basis: production-transition evidence in section 1D.

### 6. AI works in one area. We need to expand it.

**Trigger and existing assets:** An operating use case has a baseline, demonstrated benefit, an owner and service/control experience. The buyer wants additional teams, locations, use cases or workload.

**Immediate question:** What can we reuse, what changes in the new context, and where should expansion proceed?

**Practical outcome:** An expansion decision and prioritized dependencies, with validated reuse assumptions, people/control changes and renewed evidence where needed. A successful first setting does not certify the next.

**Example:** A support assistant is working in one business unit, but other units have different knowledge sources, languages, permissions and accountability.

**Include/exclude:** Include capacity growth as well as broader organizational rollout. Exclude a pilot with no dependable live evidence; exclude expansion being used to avoid fixing poor existing results.

**Clarifier:** “Can we show that the current use works in practice, or do we first need to establish or recover that evidence?” Evidence basis: scaling/foundations findings in section 1D and organizational change in 1E.

### 7. Our AI is in use, but the results are falling short.

**Trigger and existing assets:** A live deployment exists, with disappointing cost, quality, benefit, adoption or control experience. There may be usage and performance data—or a measurement gap that must be acknowledged.

**Immediate question:** Why are the results insufficient, and should we improve, redesign, replace, reduce scope or stop?

**Practical outcome:** A diagnosis against an agreed baseline and outcome, a targeted corrective decision with an owner, and a retest/stop criterion. More usage alone is not proof of value.

**Example:** Employees have an assistant but still redo its outputs; time saved in drafting is being consumed by review.

**Include/exclude:** Include low adoption, unclear responsibilities and weak measurement. Exclude simple expansion of a service already delivering the intended benefit.

**Clarifier:** “Is the cause already known—for example role design—or do we need to diagnose it?” A known work-design issue may go directly to the Human–Agent Operating Model. Evidence basis: measurement, adoption and workflow findings in sections 1C–1E.

## 5. Mapping to actual Cognirise methods—not invented capabilities

This section is a recommendation derived from the current source audit in `method-boundaries.md`. It does not change any method or canonical framework.

| Situation | Relevant existing decision work | What it actually supplies | Conditional IDAO connection |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Investment case | IDAO's existing opportunity-framing decision; AI Use-Case Prioritization when actual alternatives exist | Opportunity/value hypothesis and comparative judgment; **not** a complete financial business case | Innovate where the opportunity/evidence is unresolved; Demonstrate for a bounded hypothesis needing proof. Existing adequate evidence can support later entry. |
| 2. Competing ideas | AI Use-Case Prioritization | Comparative scorecard; Innovate/Demonstrate/Activate/Stop recommendation | Route each selected proposal on its evidence. Treat its recommendation as input to the canonical gate, not permission to bypass it. |
| 3. Existing strategy | Direct IDAO entry after reviewing existing evidence; use Prioritization, Value-to-Scale, readiness or work-design methods only for a specific unresolved decision | IDAO delivery logic; supporting methods answer their own scoped questions, not a generic “roadmap implementation score” | Demonstrate when a chosen idea needs proof; Activate when the existing evidence supports implementation; Operate for an already-live service needing operating support. Return to Innovate only where the opportunity itself must be reframed. |
| 4. Process problem | IDAO opportunity framing; Human–Agent Operating Model for defined people/agent work design; Agentic Operations Readiness for an actual proposed agent workflow | Bounded problem/evidence; roles and handovers; or Proceed/Prepare/Stop for agent workflow conditions | Innovate for unresolved intervention; Demonstrate to test it. Do not require agent-specific assessments for a non-agent solution. |
| 5. Pilot to routine use | Canonical IDAO gate; Agentic Operations Readiness if agent-based; Human–Agent Operating Model where responsibilities/adoption change | Workflow conditions and unresolved actions; roles, rights, enablement and measures | Continue Demonstrate if evidence is insufficient; enter Activate for governed implementation when justified; Operate after the required release/ownership evidence. |
| 6. Expand proven use | AI Value-to-Scale for cross-organizational constraints; Prioritization for expansion choices; readiness/work design for changed workflows | Organizational evidence gaps, comparative choices, or scoped workflow/people decisions | Operate informs expansion. New contexts can require Demonstrate or Activate; materially new opportunities can require Innovate. Not a blanket “scale approval.” |
| 7. Underperformance | IDAO operating evidence and loopback; Human–Agent Operating Model for work/adoption causes; Value-to-Scale for repeated systemic constraints; readiness for unsafe agent workflow conditions | Evidence-led corrective route; relevant roles, conditions or organizational gaps | Stay in Operate for bounded improvement; return to the responsible earlier stage when assumptions fail. Stop or constrain unsafe/unviable work; do not automatically prescribe another pilot. |

**The first arrow remains a decision, not compulsory product navigation.** Where no separate supporting method is needed, name IDAO's existing decision/gate directly. This preserves the intended logic without inventing a new framework just to fill a box.

### Important limits to communicate

AI Value-to-Scale produces an organizational maturity/evidence profile, not a financial business case. AI Use-Case Prioritization supplies comparative planning judgments, not validated cost-benefit forecasts. Agentic Operations Readiness is specifically about conditions for an agent workflow, not generic implementation readiness. Human–Agent Operating Model concerns work, responsibilities, capabilities and adoption; it is not a universal process-improvement or finance method.

A full business case may need cost modelling, benefit attribution, sensitivity analysis and funding approval beyond what is currently published. A roadmap-to-delivery engagement may need a detailed backlog, commercial choices, integration planning and resourcing. These are **honest gaps between the buyer's desired outcome and the published tools**, not authorization to add methods or claim that the existing assessments already deliver them.

IDAO and Agent Authority stay unchanged. Do not reinterpret a supporting assessment's score as satisfying IDAO's evidence gates. The canonical rule is evidence-dependent entry, not mandatory restart or automatic advancement based on possessing a document.

Agent Authority is relevant when a proposed or existing **Knowledge, Decision or Action handover** requires an authority decision. A specialist can enter it directly from any situation; it must not be hidden from someone asking who may approve or act. But it is not a generic prerequisite for comparing AI investment ideas. Value Scan can be offered as optional facilitated help to resolve an unclear starting point—not a fifth methodology or mandatory first step.

## 6. The post-strategy handoff in practical terms

The opening posture should be: **“Show us what you already have; we will establish what can move into delivery and what still needs evidence.”** This is proposed engagement language, not an existing guaranteed package.

Use the strategy, supporting research, prioritized use cases, architecture, approvals and any pilot evidence as assets. Establish which decisions are approved, which remain assumptions, and which have changed. Then identify the first bounded delivery outcome, accountable owner, acceptance measures, budget/capacity and dependencies.

| What the buyer actually has | Recommended next decision | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Direction and a long list, but no agreed priorities | Select and sequence work using Prioritization | Calling this implementation while quietly repeating the entire strategy |
| Chosen opportunity, but weak benefit/feasibility evidence | Close the specific value/evidence gap; Innovate or Demonstrate as required | Assuming roadmap inclusion proves a business case |
| Tested solution plus adequate implementation evidence | Enter Activate under the existing gate rules | Requiring a new maturity assessment or discovery phase without a reason |
| Already operating solution and a roadmap for extension | Operate-led expansion decision, with targeted retesting of changed assumptions | Treating the whole organization as a first-time adopter |

Revisit prior strategic decisions only for a material reason: changed business goals, unsupported economics, unusable data, important regulatory or risk changes, invalidated feasibility, unclear ownership that changes the proposition, or contradictory operating evidence. Missing implementation detail alone does not make the strategy worthless.

It also exposes two deliberate exceptions: specialist authority questions need direct access, and multiple concurrent initiatives cannot be reduced to one company-wide category. Those are explicit routing principles, not a claim that the labels have been validated with buyers.

## 8. Limits and confidence

**High confidence in the underlying distinctions:** identifying value is not the same as selecting among proposals; a strategy is not a deployed solution; a pilot is not a sustained service; expansion and correction need different evidence. Each conclusion draws on multiple types of evidence, including official or academic sources and credible industry research.

**Moderate confidence in this exact seven-part recommendation:** it is an editorial synthesis, not a segmentation study. There are other reasonable ways to combine investment and prioritization or to give adoption its own entry. The constructed scenarios test logical coverage, not human comprehension.

**Limited regional generalization:** GCC sources lean toward larger organizations and consulting/vendor-sponsored research. Some have small samples or incomplete sampling detail. Employee, executive and country-readiness evidence cannot be combined into a single regional buyer statistic. UAE/GCC SME-specific validation remains a gap.

**Post-consultancy evidence boundary:** two targeted search rounds found a strong planned strategy-to-implementation procurement handoff, but not a verified case of a completed consultant-authored roadmap followed by successful delivery that exactly matches the user's example. That exact use case remains a legitimate user requirement, not a measured prevalence claim.

**Freshness and causality:** older NBER, RAND, UK guidance and KPMG material is marked as historical and used only for mechanisms/context. No supplier savings or survey ROI percentage is presented as a guaranteed or independently audited result. The Alibaba preprint is a November 2025 manuscript archived under a 2026 arXiv identifier; the exact posting date was not established in the saved evidence. No changing company valuation, market-share or current supplier-ranking claims are used.

The register retains excluded sources as a transparent screening trail. Search-only snippets and preliminary headline “AI failure rate” claims were not used to establish the recommended segments. Source count is not a substitute for evidence quality.

## 9. Decisions for the user's review

My recommendation is to approve the **seven situations and their distinctions in principle**, then refine wording before any design work. Keep strategy/operations/implementation as context; keep methods behind the business need; preserve direct IDAO and specialist authority/work-design entry.

Three decisions genuinely need the user's view:

1. **Does this seven-situation set reflect the buyers Cognirise wants to serve?** In particular, keep investment-case work separate from choosing among proposals.
2. **Should people/adoption have its own eighth situation?** My recommendation is no for the general list: make it explicit within delivery, expansion and underperformance, with direct access for a known work-design need.
3. **How explicitly should the eventual offer promise business-case and roadmap-to-delivery outputs?** The need is real, but the current self-service methods do not produce a complete financial case or full delivery plan. Agree that promise before writing sales copy.

**Stop point:** This report and the accompanying brief are ready for review. No design, implementation, methodology change, CMS update or publication is authorized by these recommendations.
