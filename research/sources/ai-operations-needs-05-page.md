### Dive Brief:

- Enterprises are changing procurement practices in response to the latest waves of AI, according to [a G2 report published Wednesday](https://company.g2.com/news/buyer-behavior-in-2025?&utm_campaign=2025-buyer-behavior-report). The peer-to-peer business software review site surveyed 1,169 business decision-makers in April.
- The majority of companies have now added stricter requirements when evaluating AI-powered software compared with other technologies. Most of those surveyed said they’ve also altered research processes, with nearly 30% leaning on large language models more often than Google.
- AI is changing purchasing priorities, too. More than two-thirds said they’d pay a premium for AI capabilities, but only if vendors were able to show value or productivity gains. Nearly half of enterprise buyers switched software providers in pursuit of better AI features.

### Dive Insight:

The introduction of generative [and agentic AI](https://www.ciodive.com/news/enterprise-AI-agent-agentic-autonomous-strategy-challenges/738172/) is quickly transforming enterprise processes, from procurement to [governance](https://www.ciodive.com/news/cio-generative-ai-risk-mitigation-strategy-NIST-framework/728257/) and [cybersecurity](https://www.ciodive.com/news/enterprise-cybersecurity-AI-risk-strategy-shift-report/743755/).

While the technologies are [known for their hype](https://www.ciodive.com/news/ChatGPT-anniversary-enterprise-strategy-evolution-generative-AI/732951/), enterprises have worked to turn experimentation into [meaningful implementation](https://www.ciodive.com/news/ChatGPT-2-years-enterprise-generative-AI-strategy/733863/). But getting from pilot to production is trying.

Some companies admit to having rushed forward. Nearly 30% of IT leaders say they [invested in AI too quickly](https://www.ciodive.com/news/IT-CIO-AI-regret-investment-experimentation-asana/747683/), up 7% from last year, according to a recent Asana survey.

CIOs and technology leaders have also [admitted to facing pressure](https://www.ciodive.com/news/enterprise-generative-AI-ROI-pilot-fail-Informatica/739485/) to move projects along faster, despite their concerns about pilots proceeding without first addressing problems uncovered by previous initiatives.

While [there is C-suite](https://www.ciodive.com/news/ceos-double-down-on-ai-investments-despite-adoption-challenges/747946/) and [board-level enthusiasm for AI](https://www.ciodive.com/news/CIO-board-presentations-documents-AI-strategy-Gartner/730831/), vendors do their fair share of [peer-pressuring technology leaders](https://www.ciodive.com/news/cio-vendor-generative-ai-hype-transparency/721133/) into buying AI tools, too. Salesforce, for example, planned to hire up to 2,000 sales workers to [promote its Agentforce platform](https://www.ciodive.com/news/Salesforce-Agentforce-agent-AI-earnings-report/734674/).

“Don’t be pushed by vendors; it’s a pressure you need to escape from,” Ilona Hansen, VP analyst at Gartner, told CIO Dive. “Once they grasp you might be a prospect, they will literally bombard you.”

Enterprises can take a measured approach to adoption by setting up a strong foundation, from [building adequate data acumen](https://www.ciodive.com/news/AI-ready-data-strategy-CIO-approach-tips/744513/) to creating stricter evaluations of AI tools.
