# Eurostat — Use of artificial intelligence in enterprises

- **Source:** Eurostat, *Use of artificial intelligence in enterprises* (2025 statistical article based on the EU ICT usage and e-commerce in enterprises survey)
- **URL:** https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Use_of_artificial_intelligence_in_enterprises
- **Publication/article date:** 2025-12-11 (official search result date)
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-14
- **Tier:** Tier 1 — official European statistical agency and harmonised enterprise survey

## Design and sample

- The article says its results are based on the **2024 EU survey on ICT usage and e-commerce in enterprises**.
- National statistical authorities conducted the survey in the first months of **2025**.
- In 2025, **157,000 of 1.53 million EU enterprises** were surveyed. The population composition was approximately **83% small, 14% medium and 3% large**.
- Coverage was enterprises with **10 or more employees and self-employed persons**. Eurostat defines small as 10–49, medium as 50–249 and large as 250 or more employees/self-employed persons.

## Evidence relevant to SME nonadopters and buyers

### Adoption remains size-skewed

In 2025, **19.95%** of EU enterprises in the covered population used at least one AI technology. By size:

- **17.00%** of small enterprises used AI;
- **30.36%** of medium enterprises used AI; and
- **55.03%** of large enterprises used AI.

Eurostat says the gap may reflect implementation complexity, economies of scale and costs. Those explanations are presented as possible reasons, not as a causal decomposition.

### Reasons for not using AI after consideration

Among EU enterprises that had ever considered using AI but did not use it, Eurostat reports:

- lack of relevant expertise: **70.89%**;
- lack of clarity about legal consequences: **52.52%**;
- concerns about violation of data protection and privacy: **48.83%**; and
- AI technologies considered not useful for the enterprise: **20.68%**.

The denominator matters: these are not all non-adopters. They are enterprises that had considered AI and then did not use it. “Not useful” is the clearest official measure here of a no-need/no-AI-alternative concern, but it is not a test of whether a specified non-AI option would produce better value.

## Buyer-need synthesis (inference, not a direct Eurostat claim)

The Eurostat pattern suggests that an SME-facing offer should make four things easier to decide:

1. whether a real use exists for this enterprise rather than merely for the sector;
2. what expertise, training and external help would be required;
3. what legal, privacy and data-protection consequences must be handled; and
4. whether the expected value exceeds a simpler non-AI solution.

The size gradient also argues against assuming that a large-enterprise implementation model transfers directly to small firms.

## Limitations and evidence boundary

- The survey covers enterprises with at least 10 employees/self-employed persons; micro businesses below that threshold are outside this article's population.
- “Reasons for not using” is conditional on having considered AI. It cannot be used as the percentage of all non-adopters with each barrier.
- The article reports association/descriptive prevalence, not causal effects, buyer willingness to pay, ROI or business-case approval.
- The size results are EU aggregates and should not be treated as country-level or global SME rates.
- Eurostat notes that unreliable data are included in calculation of European aggregates and that article values can differ from the database after updates.
