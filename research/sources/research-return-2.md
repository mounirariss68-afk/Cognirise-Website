Key Facts
- Saved 6 fetched evidence pages plus search-only snippets and a validated 6-entry registry. No app/CMS changes, contacts, workflows, or previews were used.
- Evidence-backed buyer situation A (synthesis, not prevalence): teams have explored/piloted AI and may already have shadow/personal-tool use, but cannot connect a narrow workflow to measurable P&L. Trigger = pressure to justify experimentation; existing assets = observed task usage, generic-tool pilots, workflow/operational data; blocked decision = fund/scale what and how to measure it; desired outcome = one owned workflow with an outcome baseline and defensible value test. MIT NANDA reports >80% explored/piloted general tools but 5% of task-specific tools reached production in its sample; it specifically records procurement difficulty quantifying indirect productivity gains.
- Evidence-backed buyer situation B (user-mandated premise plus research-supported execution gap): a buyer already has a consulting strategy/roadmap and needs the next operational move. The fetched research does not prove consulting purchase; it supports the post-strategy problem: ServiceNow/Oxford Economics reports only 19% operationalizing AI impact against business goals, while MBS argues task-level redesign, decision rights, capability pathways, and outcome measures are required. Trigger = roadmap handoff; existing assets = roadmap/vision (premise), governance and process artifacts; blocked decision = which owner/workflow/data/metric moves first; desired outcome = convert roadmap into a governed, measurable first workflow. Distinguishing A vs B: A is value/problem discovery; B has direction chosen and needs execution sequencing/proof.
- Real operational comparator: Alibaba’s 5,940-agent randomized test found faster issue identification and better perceived quality but no significant retrial change; gains concentrated among lower performers, while top performers’ quality declined with more concurrent-chat shift-away time. Deployment should therefore be segmented, not one-size-fits-all.
- Procurement trigger: CIO Dive’s May 2025 report of G2’s 1,169 decision-makers says AI evaluation requirements tightened; >2/3 would pay a premium only for demonstrated value/productivity, and nearly half switched providers for better AI features.

Notable Claims Requiring Cross-Reference
- MIT’s 95% zero-return/5% production figures are directional, self-reported/public-disclosure based, anonymized, and not prevalence or causal estimates.
- ServiceNow is vendor-sponsored (Tier3); UAE findings lack country sample detail. GCC search snippets are retained but regional execution claims require the underlying ServiceNow country data.
- NBER’s 14%/34% field results are a working paper, one firm, and older than 18 months as of 2026-09-14.

- Tier2: CIO Dive/G2 buyer survey report; Melbourne Business School/IDIA whitepaper and roundtable.

Gaps & Unanswered Questions

- Need direct evidence on post-consulting roadmap handoff, workflow-owner selection, baseline metrics, and GCC country-specific samples. Fairmarkit fetch returned only “Explore” and was excluded from the registry.
