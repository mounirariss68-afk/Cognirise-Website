## Key Facts

- Research date: **2026-09-14**. I ran six independent searches, including two GCC-focused Arabic/English searches, then fetched three primary/government pages. No app, CMS, or workflow changes were made.
- **Buyer situation A — “We are exploring AI, but cannot get an investment case approved.”** Evidence supports this as a recognizable starting problem: prior firm surveys reviewed by OECD explicitly ask about difficulty finding appropriate use cases, proving added value over alternative methods, skills, integration, data, cost, security, and funding [OECD, `ai-business-case-01-oecd.md`, lines 45–58]. The trigger is pressure/interest to invest without a defensible choice. Existing assets to verify are candidate use-case ideas, a baseline workflow/problem, relevant data and systems, strategic goals, and a cost/benefit hypothesis—not merely an AI technology shortlist. The blocked decision is “which problem, if any, should receive money and why AI is better than a non-AI option?” The desired outcome is a decision-ready first investment or an evidence-backed stop/pivot, not another list of AI possibilities.
- **Buyer situation B — “We already bought a consulting strategy/roadmap; what happens next?”** This is a distinct user-mandated entry situation, but I found no fetched source that measures its prevalence or documents a named buyer. Treat it as a constructed but highly specific buyer case, not validated market incidence. The existing asset is the purchased strategy/roadmap and its stated priorities. The trigger is the handoff from advisory work to funding, ownership, sequencing, and delivery. The blocked decision is which roadmap item becomes the first resourced initiative, with what baseline, success measure, data/integration work, risk controls, owner, and go/no-go gate. The desired outcome is an approved, staged, accountable next step (or an explicit decision to revise the roadmap), not a second strategy.
- The clearest discriminator between A and B is the **decision artifact already in hand**: A lacks a validated, decision-ready case and may not know which use case to pursue; B has a strategy/roadmap but lacks execution evidence, resourcing, and governance. A useful buyer question is: “Do you already have a leadership-approved strategy/roadmap with prioritized initiatives, owners, costs, dependencies, and measures?” A “no” points to A; a “yes, but nothing is funded or moving” points to B.
- CSIRO’s June 2025 guide provides evidence for the practical handoff needed in both cases: define the business problem first; test measurable value, full lifecycle costs, data quality/governance, integration, risk, and whether a non-AI option is better [CSIRO, `ai-business-case-02-csiro.md`, lines 11–24 and 42–54]. It defines AI projects as scoped activities with timelines, metrics, and resource allocation rather than casual experimentation [lines 68–78].
- For uncertain investments, CSIRO recommends staged/iterative evaluation and portfolio review, including decisions to **continue, pivot, or stop** as information changes [CSIRO, lines 255–276 and 277–295]. That directly distinguishes an exploration/business-case need from a post-roadmap execution need: the former needs evidence to select; the latter needs gates and accountability to activate.
- OECD’s 2025 publication is useful for buyer-language discovery, but its annex is a comparison of selected earlier surveys. It shows the kinds of questions organizations face; it does **not** establish 2025 prevalence or causal failure rates [OECD, lines 16, 45–58].
- New Zealand’s MBIE announced on 2025-07-08 that its AI strategy was released alongside voluntary responsible-AI guidance for businesses, described as practical actions to support safe, successful adoption [MBIE, `ai-business-case-03-nz-mbie.md`, lines 15–35]. This supports the need for responsible adoption steps after interest, but not a specific ROI template or buyer case.

## Gaps & Unanswered Questions

- No source fetched here gives a named organization’s full before/after investment decision: buyer role, budget, alternatives considered, approval gate, and outcome.
- No source validates the exact “strategy/roadmap already purchased, now needs next step” situation. This should remain a distinct hypothesis for user validation, not be presented as a measured market segment.
- Evidence does not tell us which artifacts buyers usually possess at either entry point (business baseline, data inventory, vendor proposal, roadmap, budget envelope, risk assessment, or pilot evidence).
- There is no reliable evidence here on how often organizations fund, pause, pivot, or stop AI initiatives, nor on time-to-approval or decision thresholds. Do not add ROI, savings, prevalence, or failure percentages beyond the bounded source claims above.
- GCC evidence is thin: the WAM snippet identifies UAE/Saudi/Qatar leaders but gives no method; the Arabic IBM result is promotional. Independent Arabic-language government/procurement or organizational accounts remain needed.
- OECD’s barrier evidence is valuable for wording but is not current incidence evidence; the underlying survey examples are largely from 2020–2021. Search-only sources with unknown dates cannot be confidently checked against the 18-month freshness cutoff.
