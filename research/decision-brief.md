# AI starting situations: decision brief

**14 September 2026 · Recommendation for review · Nothing implemented**

## Recommendation

Keep **situation → relevant decision method → IDAO**, but start with the business decision—not the name, scope or terminology of a method. Recommend seven situations:

| Proposed situation | What the organization needs next |
|---|---|
| **We need to know where AI is worth investing.** | Establish a useful opportunity and credible business case, including benefits, full costs, alternatives and evidence. “Not AI” or “not yet” must remain valid outcomes. |
| **We have several AI ideas and need to choose.** | Compare proposals and decide what to fund, investigate, sequence or stop. |
| **We have an AI strategy and need to implement it.** | Use the existing roadmap to define owned, funded delivery work; close actual gaps rather than repeat strategy by default. |
| **We need to improve a specific process.** | Understand the operational problem and determine whether AI, other automation or process changes would help. |
| **We have a pilot and need to put it into everyday use.** | Establish what remains before a tested solution can become dependable, integrated and supported normal work. |
| **AI works in one area. We need to expand it.** | Decide what can be reused and what must change for other teams, locations, languages or workloads. |
| **Our AI is in use, but the results are falling short.** | Diagnose benefit, quality, cost or adoption problems and decide whether to improve, redesign, replace or stop. |

These are recommended descriptions, **not customer quotations or validated market segments**.

## What the research changes

**Investment case and prioritization should remain distinct.** The UK government's business research identifies absent business need and skills as important barriers; CSIRO's guidance explicitly evaluates problem fit, value, costs and non-AI alternatives. Establishing that an investment makes sense is different from deciding which of several proposals comes first. [[1]](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-adoption-research/ai-adoption-research) [[2]](https://www.csiro.au/-/media/D61/Investing-in-AI-projects-guide/2025-Evaluating-and-prioritising-AI-projects-.pdf) The OECD's review of firm surveys also distinguishes appropriate use cases, value and other adoption barriers. [[3]](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/the-adoption-of-artificial-intelligence-in-firms_f9ef33c3-en/full-report/comparisons-among-recent-ai-surveys_6577dea3.html)

**An existing roadmap deserves its own entry.** London CIV's implementation tender explicitly refers to a separately procured AI strategy programme and seeks implementation, integration, governance, training and support. This is evidence of a planned handoff—not proof of a completed consultant strategy or successful implementation. [[4]](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/057908-2026) The user's completed-consultant-roadmap scenario is retained as a specific requirement; its market prevalence was not measured.

**A pilot, a live service and expansion need different decisions.** RAND identifies practical failure mechanisms around problem understanding, data and operational infrastructure; Cisco examines scaling and measurement constraints; GCC research distinguishes adoption, wider deployment and value realization. These sources support the distinction, not universal failure or ROI percentages. [[5]](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA2680-1.html) [[6]](https://www.cisco.com/c/dam/m/en_us/solutions/ai/readiness-index/2025-m10/documents/cisco-ai-readiness-index-2025-realizing-the-value-of-ai.pdf) [[7]](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai-in-gcc-countries-in-pursuit-of-scale-and-value)

## How this connects to the current methods

- **Prioritization** compares opportunities; it is not a complete financial business-case tool.
- **Value-to-Scale** identifies organizational capability/evidence gaps; it should not be the default response to “help us justify an investment.”
- **Agentic Operations Readiness** tests conditions for a proposed agent workflow—not all AI or all implementation.
- **Human–Agent Operating Model** addresses changed work, roles, rights, capability and adoption. Make this explicit in relevant situations, with direct access for an already-known work-design need.
- **IDAO** can be entered directly when existing evidence supports it. A purchased strategy does not force a restart, and a roadmap or score does not bypass an evidence gate.
- **Agent Authority** remains directly accessible for a consequential handover question, without becoming a mandatory step for every buyer.

There is an honest offer gap: a full financial business case and a detailed roadmap-to-delivery plan require work beyond the published self-service methods. Do not imply those outputs already come from an assessment.

## Decisions to review

1. **Approve the seven distinctions in principle?** I recommend keeping investment-case work separate from choosing among proposals.
2. **Keep strategy / operations / implementation as context rather than exclusive categories?** I recommend yes: a buyer can have all three concerns at once.
3. **Give adoption its own eighth entry?** I recommend not initially. Make it explicit within delivery, expansion and underperformance, while preserving direct work-design access.

The full report includes detailed boundaries, method/IDAO mappings, the post-strategy handoff, all six current-to-proposed changes, 14 constructed scenarios and evidence limitations.

**No design, website, CMS, methodology or publication changes have been made. User review comes next.**
